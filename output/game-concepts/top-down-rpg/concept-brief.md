# Top-down RPG concepts for iPad and iPhone
Audience: ages 9–12. Direction: a substantial adventure inspired by the appeal of Pokémon—an explorable world, a starter companion, team building, rivals, turn-based battles, discoveries and persistent progression. All names are working titles. These images are generated visual concepts, not playable builds.

## Wildbound
Leave a coastal hometown with one creature and explore an island region whose ancient observatories are waking up. Recruit and evolve a team, challenge rival explorers, and discover why wild creatures are changing.
The hook: companions have useful field abilities as well as battle moves. A plant creature cuts vines; a stone creature breaks cracked walls; an aquatic creature opens river routes. Older areas gain new secrets as the team develops.
Battle direction: compact three-creature teams, one active creature per side, four moves per creature, elemental matchups and turn-based switching.
Opening sequence: choose a starter, meet the rival, cross Mossfall Route, discover the vine-sealed observatory, find a companion ability, open a shortcut, and fight a rival before the first dungeon.
First playable chapter: one hometown, one forest route, one observatory dungeon, a rival, 12 creatures and one boss encounter.

## Scrapwild
An abandoned machine frontier has become home to wild robots. Explore workshop towns and overgrown factories, defeat or befriend rogue machines, and assemble a team from recovered parts.
The hook: choose each robot's chassis, movement system and tool. Parts change battle roles and how robots interact with the overworld; a coil opens a power gate while a magnet moves salvage.
Battle direction: turn-based robot duels where heat, charge and part combinations create tactical tradeoffs.
Opening sequence: repair a small companion, explore Rustwater Outpost, recover a power core, open the north gate and enter the first factory ruin.
First playable chapter: one workshop town, one salvage route, one factory dungeon, a rival mechanic and a small deliberately limited set of interchangeable parts.
Mockup: scrapwild.png

## Afterbell
After school, the familiar town reveals hidden spirit passages. Befriend a team of strange spirits, duel rival spirit-keepers, and investigate why ordinary places are changing.
The hook: the town has connected everyday and spirit-world maps. Companions reveal different clues or crossings, making familiar alleys, shops and playgrounds worth revisiting.
Battle direction: turn-based spirit duels with short status combinations and character-specific abilities.
Opening sequence: meet a strange companion outside school, follow a lantern trail, discover a spirit doorway and solve a first neighborhood mystery.
First playable chapter: one neighborhood, its spirit-world counterpart, one mystery dungeon, a rival and 10 spirits.
Mockup: afterbell.png

## Mobile presentation
Landscape on both iPad and iPhone is the initial recommendation for readable map exploration. Use a virtual stick plus contextual interaction, with optional tap-to-move considered during prototyping. Battles should use large touch move cards. Rework exact HUD scale for each device instead of shrinking the tablet layout. Support interruption-friendly saves.
No runtime, device usability, content volume or performance claims have been validated by these images.

## Recommendation
Wildbound is the closest match to the requested Pokémon-style adventure. Scrapwild gives customization the strongest role. Afterbell provides the clearest neighborhood mystery and narrative identity.
