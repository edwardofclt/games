# Wobble Stack — Story and Plot

## Premise

At a rooftop jelly factory, a delivery machine has been left running. Soft, colorful blocks keep arriving, sliding back and forth above a narrow platform.

The night-shift operator, Wobble, has one solution: build up.

The blocks are springy, the platform is small, and every slightly crooked placement makes the next one more interesting. Wobble has decided to see how high this ridiculous tower can go.

## Plot

The first block drops onto the platform with a satisfying squash. The second lands on top. For a moment, this seems like an excellent storage system.

Then a block lands slightly off-center. The stack leans, wobbles, and settles. Another block is already sliding overhead.

Wobble keeps building. Each landing adds a point and makes the tower a little taller. A careful drop can steady a shaky-looking stack; a careless one can leave it balancing on an edge. The suspense comes from watching the result of a decision that took only a tap.

Sooner or later, a block misses or the tower loses its balance. The stack collapses into a soft, colorful tumble. The machine pauses, and the final height is recorded as the run's score.

The platform clears. A fresh block slides into position.

Wobble is convinced the next tower will be straighter.

## How the story appears in play

- The opening view contains the platform, one moving block, and the instruction **“Tap to drop.”**
- Wobble is the implied operator controlled by the player; a visible character is optional.
- Squash, stretch, and settling movements give the blocks personality.
- Each successfully placed block adds one point. The camera follows the rising stack so its top stays readable.
- A collapse is the comic payoff. A short tumble leads into the score and a quick restart.

## Tone and boundaries

Tactile, absurd, and satisfying. The humor comes from an increasingly improbable tower and the player's confidence that it can survive one more block.

Reaching the clouds is a visual ambition, not a fixed ending or an additional objective. The factory is background context; playing requires no production management, materials, upgrades, or story screens. The tower itself tells the story of each attempt.
